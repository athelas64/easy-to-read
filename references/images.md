# Pictures for easy-to-read texts: what to ask for, and how to brief it

Many readers with intellectual disabilities read slowly, or read only some of the words.
A picture next to a paragraph tells them what the paragraph is about before they read
it, and helps them find that part again later. That is why the European standards ask
for images next to the text (Inclusion Europe, standards 34 to 39) and why the Czech
methodology lists symbol databases and own photographs among the required aids.

The writer usually cannot supply the pictures: the customer owns the building, the
form, the counter and the staff. So the deliverable is an **image brief** the customer
can act on. This file says what goes into it.

## What to picture

A picture stands for one concrete noun or one action from the paragraph. The test: can
the reader point at the picture and say the paragraph's key word?

| Kind | Examples | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| The thing | the ID card, the front page of the form, the birth certificate, money, a key, a phone | the reader recognises what to bring or what they will receive |
| The place | the entrance of the office, the counter number, the door sign, the waiting room | on the day, the reader matches the picture to the real place |
| The person | the official behind the counter, a doctor, a bank clerk: adults, shown by role | says who the reader will talk to |
| The action | handing over papers, signing, phoning, queueing, putting a letter in a postbox, clicking a button | shows what the reader does |
| Time and cost | a calendar with the date circled, a clock at the opening hour, coins and notes for a fee | numbers are hard, the picture carries them |
| The outcome | the new card in a hand, a letter arriving, a stamp on the form | shows what "done" looks like |

Prioritise, in this order: what the reader must do, what they must bring, where and
when, what happens next, what happens if they do nothing. Background explanation ("the
law says") needs no picture.

**The customer's own photographs beat everything else** for places, forms and staff:
the reader will see the same door, the same counter, the same form. Ask for them first.
Get consent from any person photographed, and prefer role over identity (a hand with
the form, not a named clerk).

## Type and style

- **Photographs**: one clear subject, plain background, daylight, nothing else to look at
  (standard 37 shows a clear photo of a man reading a book against a busy one). Adults.
  Local look: a Slovak office, Slovak documents, a Slovak postbox. Foreign stock imagery
  (a US mailbox, a UK passport) confuses.
- **Drawings and symbols**: one symbol set for the whole document, adult in style, the
  same line weight. Symbol sets used in augmentative communication (ARASAAC and the
  like) are known to many readers with intellectual disabilities, which is a real
  advantage; readers who never used them treat them as any other drawing.
- **The same picture for the same thing** throughout (standard 38). Keep a picture
  dictionary for the document: term, picture file, where it appears. Hand it to the
  customer with the brief so later pages stay consistent.
- **No metaphors**: no light bulb for "idea", no hourglass for "wait", no shaking hands
  for "agreement", unless the reader check shows the readers know them.
- **No text in pictures** except a real label the reader will see on the day (the door
  sign "Podateľňa"). Screenshots only when the reader will use that very screen; crop to
  the one control and enlarge it.
- **Never** images for children in a text for adults (standard 36), clip art, cluttered
  scenes, decorative icons, flags and logos as illustrations, pictures behind text, text
  over pictures, or a picture as the only link to something.
- **Size and place**: next to the paragraph it explains, left of it or above it; one or
  two per paragraph at most (standard 39); in print at least 3 to 4 cm high; on screen at
  least about 120 px, larger on phones. The picture never replaces the sentence.
- **Colour**: fine, but the picture must still read in black and white (people photocopy
  easy-to-read leaflets) and must not rely on colour alone to carry meaning.

## Alt text and captions

- On the web, in Word and in PDF every picture gets an alternative text: what it shows,
  in the same plain words as the text, short. "Občiansky preukaz." "Žena podáva papiere
  úradníčke." No "picture of", no sentence-long descriptions.
- When a picture only repeats the sentence beside it, an empty alternative text (a
  decorative image) spares a screen-reader user hearing everything twice. Choose one
  approach per document and say in the brief which one.
- Easy-to-read texts rarely need captions: the sentence next to the picture is the
  caption. If a caption is unavoidable, it is one short sentence.

## The image brief: the table to hand over

One row per picture, in the language of the customer:

| Section or paragraph | What the picture shows | Type | Source suggestion | Alt text | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Čo si vziať so sebou | an ID card, front side, on a plain surface | photo | customer's own, or a specimen from the Ministry | Občiansky preukaz. | the same photo everywhere the card is mentioned |
| Kam ísť | the entrance of the office with its sign | photo | customer's own | Vchod do klientskeho centra na Hodžovej ulici. | daytime, no people |
| Čo urobíte na úrade | a person handing papers over a counter | drawing | ARASAAC "entregar documentos" | Človek podáva papiere úradníčke. | licence NC: confirm use |
| Koľko to stojí | coins and one banknote | photo | stock, or own | Peniaze. | keep the same money picture for every fee |
| Kedy | a calendar with one day circled | drawing | one symbol set | Kalendár. | no real date in the picture |

Above the table, three lines: the style chosen (photos, one symbol set, or both and how
they are split), the total number of pictures, and who supplies what by when. Below it,
the picture dictionary (same picture for the same thing) and a sentence saying that the
text works without pictures and they can be added later, so missing pictures never
delay publishing the text.

## The easy-to-read mark

The mark is not an illustration and is not placed like one. The European easy-to-read
logo may be used only after readers with intellectual disabilities have checked the
text, together with the sentence "© European Easy-to-Read Logo: Inclusion Europe. More
information at www.easy-to-read.eu". Until then the text carries the line "Tento text
je napísaný podľa európskych pravidiel pre ľahko čitateľné texty" and a link to the
standards page. Details in `customer-package.md`, section "Labelling".
